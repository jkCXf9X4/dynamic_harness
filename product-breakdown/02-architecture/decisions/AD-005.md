# AD-005: Founding Boundary-Scoped, Participation Universal

## Status
Accepted

## Layer
Architecture

## Context
Question: is child layer-by-layer collaboration an *orchestration* feature
(only orchestrators may form teams) or a *general capability every agent
possesses*? Hackman's five conditions — especially #1 (real team) and #2
(compelling direction) — and Ostrom's principle #8 (nested enterprises) are
the ground the answer sits on.

Documented in:
- `product-breakdown/02-architecture/multi-agent-coordination/INVESTIGATION.md`
  §"Scope: orchestration feature or general capability?" and
  §"Codified: one distributed capability, two facets, structure-driven
  activation".

**Rationale basis:** DOCUMENTED — the split (founder vs member) and the
structural-activation design are stated explicitly with Hackman/Ostrom
citations.

## Decision
The collaboration capability is split along the two halves the theory already
separates — **leader sets conditions, members execute** (Hackman) and
**mechanisms are institutions, not persons** (Ostrom):

- **Founding is boundary-scoped.** Any **node with children** — at that layer —
  may found a team: declare membership (`collaborate_with` / `introduce`),
  post the team charter (direction + acceptance), open the team workspace,
  arbitrate conflicts. Only the parent has the decomposition view and the
  authority to bound membership; a leaf cannot found a team it cannot see.
- **Participation is universal.** **Every agent, at any depth, leaves
  included**, may act as a team member: read/write the team workspace, bounded
  messages, negotiate, escalate. Interdependence is not depth-dependent; two
  leaf siblings holding half a result are exactly a Hackman "real team".

Codified as one `CollaborationCapability` object on every `Agent` node with two
facets — MEMBER (active iff a parent introduced you) and FOUNDER (active iff
you have children) — where **activation is structural, not role-based**: no
class split, no role string, nothing to promote. Nested enterprises compose
"layer by layer" by construction.

## Alternatives Considered
- **Orchestration-only (only orchestrators may collaborate)** — REJECTED:
  interdependence is not depth-dependent; forcing every collaborating leaf to
  delegate would grow the tree (each handoff becomes a delegation), against
  the "prefer broad, flat, shallow trees" discipline.
- **General founding (any agent may introduce arbitrary others)** — REJECTED:
  would require global addressing and global authority — the exact
  unconstrained-B shape rejected in AD-001/AD-002.
- **Role-tag-based activation** — REJECTED: founding self-restricts to nodes
  that *have* children (`self.children`), which is cleaner than a role string
  and scales with delegation by construction.

## Consequences
**Positive:**
- Collaboration scales with delegation by construction: every subtree that
  spawns interdependent children gets a team; independent fan-out needs none.
- Leaves that can hand off via the team workspace stay leaves — trees stay
  shallow.
- Nested enterprises (Ostrom #8) compose: a node is simultaneously a member of
  its parent's team and, if it has children, the founder of its own nested
  team — the same capability object, both sides idle until structure activates
  them.
- Distribution ≠ privilege: member writes are scoped to the team workspace;
  founder actions require `self.children` non-empty. A node with no team and no
  children holds the object but no active workspace — inert, not powerful.

**Negative:**
- The founding decision still lives at each boundary: uniform machinery changes
  *where the code lives*, not *who decides membership* — the parent of each
  layer remains the authority (this is intended, but it means the capability is
  not fully self-organizing).
- The collaboration setting (charter, workspace, norms) must be instantiated
  per layer — the workspace mechanics and cadence semantics are still open
  (IMP-002).
